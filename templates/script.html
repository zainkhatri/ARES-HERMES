Opening~90 seconds · verbal, no slide
You're sitting down. 4 Sigma people across from you. AE is sick. You were on the discovery call. Don't re-introduce yourself or explain who Sigma is — get to their problem in sentence two.
Say

With Q4 coming up, that lag is costing you.

Your CIO put it in a way that stuck with me — 'Can Sigma make our Snowflake investment pay off for the people making decisions, not just the people maintaining pipelines?' That's what today is about. Quick slides to set the stage, then I want to show you something live on your actual data. Treat this like a working session — stop me at any point."
Don't pause. Go straight to slide 1.
Slides~3 minutes · 5 slides, move fast

Slide 1 — What We Heard

Say
"So here's what we took away from our last conversation. Three things.

Midwest is your lowest-margin region — and you don't know why. That question is sitting in a ticket queue right now.

Your BI team is fielding 30 to 40 ad-hoc requests a week. Merchandisers wait days for answers, sometimes longer.

And Q4 sourcing decisions are being made on data that's 48 hours old. Last November, a supplier disruption hit your best-selling TV line and by the time the report came back, the buying decision had already been made."
Ask — Merchandiser
"Does that reflect what your team was dealing with going into last Q4?"
Wait for the answer. Nod. Then: "That's exactly what I want to show you." Move to slide 2.

Slide 2 — Current vs. Future State

Say
"So today — your merchandiser has a question, files a ticket, waits four days, gets an answer that might already be stale. Her decision is reactive at best.

With Sigma — same question, answered live, in the meeting, on data that's current as of right now. No ticket, no wait. And when the decision gets made, it doesn't disappear into Slack — it writes back into the data. Auditable, timestamped, visible to the whole team.

The workflow changes. The data infrastructure doesn't."

Slide 3 — Sigma and the CDW

Say
"One thing I want your data team to hear before we get into the product. Sigma doesn't move your data. Doesn't copy it, doesn't store it anywhere. It runs a live query directly on Snowflake, inherits your existing permissions, result shows up in the browser. Every query is in your warehouse history. Your governance doesn't change."
20 seconds max. Aimed at the Head of BI. Don't dwell — next slide immediately.

Slide 4 — Demo Roadmap

Say
"Here's what I'm going to show you. Three things. First — your merchandiser building her own analysis on live Snowflake data, no SQL, no ticket. Second — AI reading 5,600 product reviews inside your Snowflake and surfacing which brands to cut for Q4. Third — that recommendation written directly back into your data warehouse. Auditable. Shared with the whole team. Let's get into it."
Don't pause. Go to the vocab slide.

Slide 5 — Sigma Syntax (30 seconds, then open Sigma)

Say
"One slide before I get into the product — this is literally all the syntax you'll see me use today.

Square brackets mean column. Operators work exactly like Excel. Functions wrap the column. And when I call an AI model, it's the same structure — function name, then what to feed it. That's it. Everything else is point and click."

Step 2 — Build COGS, Revenue, Profit live

90 seconds. Don't slow down — the point is how fast this goes, not the math itself.
Do
Click Cost column dropdown → Add New Column → type [Cost] * [Quantity] → Enter → rename COGS → click $

Click Price column dropdown → Add New Column → type [Price] * [Quantity] → Enter → rename Revenue → click $

Click Revenue dropdown → Add New Column → type [Revenue] - [COGS] → Enter → rename Profit → click $
Say — while building
"Cost times quantity — what Plugs paid for the product. Price times quantity — what they made selling it. Revenue minus COGS — profit. Same syntax as Excel.

[after Profit lands] Your merchandiser just calculated profit across 4.6 million rows. No SQL. No ticket. No waiting."
Ask — Head of BI
"How many calculation requests like this does your team field in a given week?"

Step 3 — Bar chart → Midwest is the problem → drill down

This is your biggest WB1 moment. Narrate before every click. Pause after each drill. Don't rush it.
Do
Hover over the table → child element icon (top-right of table) → Chart → Bar chart
X-axis: Store Region · Y-axis: drag Profit
Say — as the chart renders
"Profit by region. Live, right now.

[pause — let them see it]

Look at that. Midwest is your lowest performing region. Not by a little — West is doing over $450 billion in profit, Midwest is dramatically below that. That gap is your Q4 problem.

Now here's the question a merchandiser would ask in this meeting: what's dragging Midwest down? Is it one product category? One store? Before Sigma, that question goes to your BI team and comes back in four days. Watch what happens now."
Do
Right-click the Midwest bar → Drill down… → select Product Family
Say
"Midwest only. Which product families are the problem?

[pause, let it update] You can see right away what's underperforming. Let me go one level deeper."
Do
Right-click the lowest performing product family → Drill down… → Store Name
Say
"Which specific stores? Now I want to know profit per customer — not just total store profit."
Do
Click Sum of Profit column header in the table below → update formula to Sum([Profit]) / CountDistinct([Customer Name]) → rename Avg Cust Profit
Do
Right-click the worst store → Drill down… → Customer Name → sort descending by Avg Cust Profit
Say
"There it is. Region — product family — store — ranked list of least profitable customers. Under three minutes. On 4.6 million live rows.

That's the question your merchandiser was waiting four days to answer last Q4. Here's what it looks like when she answers it herself, in the meeting."

Step 5 — Type the AI formula live

This is your second wow moment. Type slowly, say the formula out loud. Silence while it runs is fine — don't fill it.
Do
In the Product Reviews table → click Review column dropdown → Add New Column
Type: CallNumber("applications.sigma.sentiment", [Review]) → Enter
Rename to Rating · format 2 decimal places
Say — while typing
"One formula. CallNumber — applications.sigma.sentiment — column Review. That's it.

What this just did: ran a large language model against every customer review in this dataset. Inside your Snowflake. Sigma isn't sending your customer data to OpenAI, not making an external API call. This is Snowflake Cortex — native AI that lives in your warehouse. Your data never left."
Let it run. Don't talk. Look at the screen. The pause is the moment.

Panel questions — Q&A after the demo

They hold questions until after the demo. These will all come at once. Stay calm. For anything you don't know: "Why does that matter to you?" or "Let me get that answer for you after this."

Most likely
"Why not just extend our Tableau investment?"
"Tableau is great at presenting answers to questions you already know. The problem you described — your merchandiser needing an answer in the meeting, not four days later — is exactly where Tableau creates the bottleneck, not solves it. Your BI team doesn't go away with Sigma. They just stop being a helpdesk."
Most likely
"We already paid for Snowflake. Why another layer?"
"That's exactly what your CIO asked us. Snowflake is world-class at storing and processing data — it's not designed to be what a merchandiser opens every morning. Sigma is what makes the investment you already made accessible to the people who need it. You're not adding a layer — you're finally getting ROI from the one you have."
Most likely
"Why not just give our power users SQL access directly to Snowflake?"
"You could — and your data team already does. The problem is your merchandiser can't write SQL. Every question she has goes through a data scientist who has 30 other requests. Sigma doesn't replace Snowflake or your data team — it lets the business reach what's already there, without going through your BI team every time."
Likely
"Can your merchandisers actually learn this?"
"If they use Excel today, they can use Sigma tomorrow. The formula syntax is identical — that's by design. We've seen merch teams self-sufficient in a week. The bigger question is what they do with the time they get back from waiting on BI."
Likely
"How does implementation actually work?"
"Sigma connects to your existing Snowflake — no migration, no data movement. Your Snowflake admin grants access to the tables you choose, and your team is live. Typical time to first workbook is days, not months. The bigger lift is change management — getting merchandisers comfortable exploring data themselves. We help with that."
Likely
"What does pricing look like?"
"Pricing is based on the number of users and the capabilities you need. Happy to put together something specific for your team size after this — that's a conversation for me and your CIO to have together. What I can say is most customers find it ROI-positive quickly because of what they're currently spending on BI bandwidth."
If they push technically
"What SQL does Sigma actually generate? Can we see the query?"
"Yes — every query Sigma runs is visible in your Snowflake query history. It generates ANSI SQL. If you want to pop into your Snowflake console right now I can show you the history, or we can dig into this in the technical session."
